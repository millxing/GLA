The 2025-26 Lakers don’t look like a contender if you look at the numbers that usually matter. They do if you watch what happens when the game gets tight. There are teams whose seasons make sense at a glance, but the 2025-26 Lakers are not one of them.

They are 38-25, good for a .603 winning percentage. That is the profile of a clearly above-average team, the kind of club you expect to see with a comfortably positive point differential and a sturdy net rating.

Instead, the Lakers have spent most of the season looking almost perfectly ordinary by those measures. Their overall net rating sits around even (+0.4 as of March 7th). On paper, that is the statistical fingerprint of a team hovering near .500, not one sitting 13 games above it.
So what explains the gap?
![Lakers chart](/images/lal_2025-26_blog_record_vs_net.png)

The easy answer is luck. The better answer is that the Lakers have been good at winning the part of the game that matters most. Strip away garbage time, and the picture already improves. The Lakers have a -6.0 net rating  in garbage time, but a +2.2 net rating in non-garbage minutes. That is not a dominant profile, but it is a meaningful shift. Their season-long numbers are being weighed down by low-leverage stretches that matter to spreadsheets more than to the standings.

Then comes the real differentiator. In clutch time, [the Lakers have posted a +26.8 net rating over 23 games with clutch minutes](https://nbaextrapass.xyz/league-summary?scope=clutch&range=season&sort=net_rating&direction=desc). The defensive rating is better in clutch time, but the offensive rating skyrockets to 135.9, best in the league. Granted, this is a small sample size, accounting for only about 2% of all the Laker minutes played this season. Consistent with the cluth metrics, their average fourth-quarter scoring margin is +1.6, which is 5th best in the league. Taken together, those numbers describe a team that spends much of the night blending into the crowd, then turns into something much more dangerous once the game narrows and every possession begins to feel heavier.
![Lakers chart](/images/lal_2025-26_blog_fourth_quarter.png)

That seems to be the identity of this Lakers team: not overwhelming, not especially impressive, but timely. They are 9-3 in games decided by five points or fewer, the best close-game winning percentage in the league among teams with at least eight such games. They have nine comeback wins after trailing through three quarters, tied for third most in the NBA. And when they enter the fourth with a lead, they almost always finish the job: they are 28-1 when leading after three. In other words, the Lakers are not consistently controlling games. They are consistently controlling endings. That distinction matters. It is the reason their record outpaces their underlying net rating.

[The Lakers’ clearest strengths are shot-making and getting to the line.](https://nbaextrapass.xyz/contribution-analysis?team=LAL) They rank second in effective field goal percentage and third in free-throw rate. When possessions tighten, they have the kind of offensive talent with Luka and Lebron that can still generate points without needing a perfect sequence. But the rest of the profile is shakier. They have negative contributions from ball handling and defensive rebounding. This is not a team that regularly wins the possession battle. And they are 27th in effective field goal defense. They are not overwhelming opponents with structure, pressure, or second-chance volume. More often, they are surviving until their shot-making and execution can take over. That is what makes them interesting, and also what makes them difficult to trust completely.

There is a version of this story that sounds ominous: teams that live on clutch dominance are usually walking a thin line. Close-game results can regress to the mean. A few late possessions bounce the other way, and suddenly the record starts to look a lot more like the net rating. But there is another version of the story, too, and it is the one the Lakers are betting on.

In the playoffs, nobody cares what your garbage-time net rating was in January. Nobody cares whether you won the third quarter by seven if you lose the last four minutes. What matters is whether you can create a good shot when the defense is set, whether you can get one stop without fouling, whether you can handle the emotional compression of a game that has shrunk to its highest-leverage possessions. 

So far, the Lakers have answered yes to those questions more often than almost anyone. They are not steamrolling teams. They are not carrying the statistical aura of a juggernaut. They are doing something subtler and, in its own way, more revealing: they are making the last stretch of the game belong to them.

That does not guarantee anything in April. But it does explain why a team with an almost neutral full-season net rating keeps piling up wins anyway. The Lakers have not been dominant all night. They have just been dominant when the night is decided.
